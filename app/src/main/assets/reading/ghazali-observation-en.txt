Our sight for instance, perhaps the best practised of all our senses, observes a shadow, and finding it apparently stationary pronounces it devoid of movement. Observation and experience, however, show subsequently that a shadow moves not suddenly, it is true, but gradually and imperceptibly, so that it is never really motionless.

Putting it into practice — advice written for Selkie
Observe a sun-cast shadow of a fixed object now and a little later. Write what changes and what stays the same. Separate what you actually observed from what you first assumed.
