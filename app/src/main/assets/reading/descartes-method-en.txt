The four rules — translation prepared for Selkie

1. The first was never to accept anything as true unless I clearly knew it to be so: carefully avoid haste and prejudice, and include nothing in my judgments beyond what appeared so clearly and distinctly to my mind that I had no reason to doubt it.

2. The second was to divide each difficulty I examined into as many parts as possible and as necessary to solve it better.

3. The third was to conduct my thoughts in order, beginning with the simplest and easiest things to understand, then rising gradually to the more complex, even assuming an order among things that do not naturally follow one another.

4. The last was everywhere to make such complete lists and such broad reviews that I could be sure of leaving nothing out.

Putting it into practice — advice written for Selkie
Choose a concrete question. Write what you know, what still needs checking and three small steps. Start with the simplest, then review your answer for omissions.
